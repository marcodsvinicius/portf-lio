03

The challenge

Ship maintenance projects involved several teams and external contractors. Information was distributed across spreadsheets and tools without centralized communication.

An external consultancy was already mapping processes. Teams had taken part in extensive interviews, and scope continued to change. The challenge was to use this knowledge and investigate only the additional points needed to design an MVP.

04

Design decisions

Build on existing discovery

Why this decision
Repeating lengthy interviews could overburden teams already participating in process mapping.
How I applied it
Together with the client and product manager, we used the consultancy’s discovery as a starting point. I added targeted interviews with shipyard, works and finance teams.

Organize design by module

Why this decision
Scope was expanding while processes were still being defined.
How I applied it
I used Double Diamond cycles by module to structure design progress and communicate deliverables to stakeholders.

Connect flows to working conditions

Why this decision
Works tracking involved approvals, changes and updates from the field.
How I applied it
I designed integration with change management and flows for desktop and smartphone tracking in collaboration with the team.

06

User feedback and expected gains

Fit with everyday work

Comments received from users indicate that the proposal fits the teams’ actual processes.

Perceived operational gains

The team sees potential to simplify tasks and save time based on discussions with users. These perceptions are not measurements.

Solution objectives

Centralize information, make data easier to find and connect change management with works tracking.

The product is under development. Time reductions discussed so far are initial estimates based on team perceptions and user feedback. They do not represent measured results or completed adoption.

07

Lessons learned

Use existing knowledge

The consultancy’s discovery helped focus design discussions without repeating the entire investigation.

Work with evolving scope

Organizing design by module helped structure work while new needs were being discussed.

Account for different routines

Discussions with shipyard, works and finance teams brought different contexts into interface decisions.
